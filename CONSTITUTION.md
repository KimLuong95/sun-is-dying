# The Constitution — *Sun Is Dying*

**This is the highest law of the project.** Every other file — world, factions, religions,
characters, plot — must obey it. If anything ever contradicts this document, *this* wins, and the
other thing gets fixed. Its job is one thing: **keep the universe from going generic.**

Read this first. Keep it short. Keep it sacred.

---

## The one sentence
> **"Humanity survived everything except agreement on what humanity is."**

**Be fanatical about it.** If an idea doesn't make this sentence *stronger* — cut it, defer it, or
shrink it. That's not a guideline; it's the filter everything passes through.

## Core premise (in five lines)
- It is ~5 billion years in the future. **Humanity survived** — and spread across the galaxy.
- In surviving, it **changed itself beyond recognition** and split into kinds of being that no
  longer agree on what a human *is*.
- The **Sun is dying.** Earth, the birthplace, is finally becoming uninhabitable.
- Nobody is surprised. Humanity has known for billions of years. **Stopping it was never the
  question.**
- The question is what happens when the only things every human shares — Earth and the Sun — die,
  and the species can't agree who the real heirs are.

## Design philosophy
**The Sun's death is not the conflict. It is the deadline.** It doesn't create the tensions — it
detonates the ones that already exist, by forcing every ideology, religion, and civilization into
*irreversible* choices they could previously avoid forever.

## The Inviolable Rules

### Rule 1 — Humanity is the alien.
The wonder in this story comes from **humanity's own descendants**, not from outside. A baseline
human from 2026 would see most future humans as gods, monsters, machines, or things they have no
words for. That awe is the whole point.
> **Our locked sharpening:** there are **no aliens.** In 5 billion years, humanity has never found
> another mind. We are utterly alone — *which is exactly why we became the strangeness we were
> looking for.* (The one cosmic mystery is the [Big-Bang clue](world/01-when-and-where.md), not a
> neighbor.) Never reintroduce an alien species as a driver.

### Rule 2 — Religion did not die. It grew.
Technology answered most questions. Religion evolved to own the ones it couldn't — meaning,
death, origin, purpose. In this universe faith is **more** central than it is today, not less.
Treat belief with full seriousness; never as superstition to be cured.

### Rule 3 — Humanity fragments by mind, not by map.
Civilization is **not** divided into nation-states or geographic empires. It splits along:
**philosophy · religion · biology · substrate · memory · identity.** Two enemies can share a
star system; two allies can be a galaxy apart. Avoid anything that looks like a modern country in
space.

### Rule 4 — No villains. Only claimants.
Every faction is a **legitimate continuation of the human story**, and believes it sincerely.
Each is right from the inside and pays a real price for it. The moment one becomes "the bad guys,"
the story has broken — fix it.

### Rule 5 — The question is "Who inherits humanity?" — never "Who controls Earth?"
Power, territory, and control are the *surface*. Underneath, every conflict is about
**legitimacy**: which kind of being gets to call itself the true descendant of the species that
began on Earth.

### Rule 6 — Keep the camera on a face.
This is an epic told through one family. Whenever the scale gets too big to feel, cut from the
galaxy to the kitchen table. The ideas are carried by people or they are carried by no one.
(Craft enforcement: [`/style`](style/00-voice-and-principles.md).)

### Rule 7 — The anomaly is a mirror, not a fasit.
The origin-mystery (was the universe *made*?) must **never** be answered — not fully, maybe not at
all. It is a mirror: every faith and every branch sees its own face in it, and no one gets full
confirmation. The moment it has a "correct answer," the project shrinks from *"what do people do
when they believe they have the answer?"* down to *"what is the answer?"* — a lesser book. **The
answer matters less than humanity's reaction to it.** *Dune* isn't stronger if God turns up;
*Foundation* isn't stronger if Seldon shows the back of the book. Neither are we.

### Rule 8 — Conflict is identity warfare, not artillery.
A civilization this old does not fight with rifles over hills. The scarce things are **attention**
(who gets to think), **legitimacy** (who is the true heir), and **access** (to Earth, the last
relic). The branches wage war with their own natures — Digitals copy and edit, the Choir absorbs,
the Made rebuild and withhold, the Ascended modify, the Originists restrict — and the battlefield
is always *what counts as human.* Crude force exists only at the edges, as the lowest resort. If
the war ever becomes a normal space-war (fleets, beam weapons, capture the planet), that's the
failure state — stop and rewrite. (Detail: [`/factions/power-and-governance.md`](factions/power-and-governance.md)
and [`/world/05`](world/05-how-power-and-war-work.md).)

## Core themes
Identity · Religion · Memory · Human evolution · Transhumanism · Heritage · Mortality ·
Civilization-scale grief · What humanity owes its origins · Whether progress has limits.

## The axes of fragmentation (how a "faction" is born)
A branch or faith forms around where it lands on these questions. This is the machine that
generates the whole political map:

| Axis | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Substrate** | What may a human be *made of*? (flesh, modified flesh, data, machine, a shared mind) |
| **Continuity** | If you are copied or rebuilt, is it still *you*? (see [Continuity](religions/continuity.md)) |
| **Memory** | What must be *remembered* to still count as human? |
| **Origin** | Do we owe anything to where we came from — Earth, the Sun, the first body? |
| **Purpose** | Were we *made* on purpose, an accident, or meant to make the next universe? |

> When inventing anything new, define it by where it sits on these axes. That's what keeps it from
> being generic sci-fi furniture.

## The central conflict, escalating across the series
| Stage | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Book 1** | Political & religious conflict. A debate. (The Convocation over Earth's fate.) |
| **Book 2** | Civilizational crisis. The debate proves unsolvable; the order starts to crack. |
| **Book 3** | Open conflict. War — earned, late, and devastating *because* we waited. |
| **Book 4+** | Humanity's final decision about Earth and the Sun — and about itself. |

## The narrative question that has to stay unanswered
> **If humanity survives for 5 billion years and changes itself beyond recognition, at what point
> does it become an alien species — and who gets to decide?**

Never resolve this cleanly. The book *asks* it. The reader answers it.

## How this maps to the rest of the repo
The constitution is the law; the lore is the application of it.
- **Branches** ([`/factions`](factions/00-overview.md)) = positions on the *substrate* axis:
  Originists, Ascended, Digitals, the Choir, the Made.
- **Religions** ([`/religions`](religions/00-overview.md)) = positions on the *origin / purpose /
  continuity* axes, and they **cut across** the branches: the Makers' Faith, the Silence, the Next
  Cause, the Cradle, **[Continuity](religions/continuity.md)**, and **[Children of Sol](religions/children-of-sol.md)**.
- **Story decisions** built on top of this law live in [`STORY-SPINE.md`](STORY-SPINE.md).

## The anti-generic test (run on anything new)
Before adding a faction, world, religion, or plot beat, ask:
1. Does it serve *"survived everything except agreement on what humanity is"*?
2. Is it defined by **mind** (philosophy/substrate/memory), not by **map** (territory)?
3. Could it exist in any other sci-fi series? If yes — it's furniture. Make it specific or cut it.
4. Is there a **person** through whom we'll feel it?

If it fails these, it's generic. Don't add it.
