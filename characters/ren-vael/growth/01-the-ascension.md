# The Ascension — *(Ren's foundational beat)*
**When:** he left home at sixteen; the first modifications began within the year. Decades before
the Convocation.
**One line:** the boy who was never quite enough for the house he was born in went away and made
himself more — and found out what "more" costs.

### Before
Ren was the quick one, and quick was never the point. In the Custodian house the virtues were
patience, keeping, staying — and he was a boy who touched everything to make it his and wanted to
be *seen* doing it. His mother loved him completely and approved of him almost never; the
distinction was lost on a sixteen-year-old. The Meritocracy's recruiters didn't flatter him. They
just tested him, told him his ceiling, and told him it could be removed. No one had ever spoken
to him about his ceiling before. At home they only ever spoke about the walls.

### The moment
He left without a fight, which was worse than a fight. The first changes were small — sleep he no
longer needed, fear that arrived and then politely waited for instructions. He wrote home once.
He sent a star chart he had computed himself, unaided, in his head, with a note: *I'm fine, the
chart proves it.* He believed that. He believed the chart was a love letter. It took him thirty
years to understand his mother had opened the envelope, found mathematics, and cried.

### After
Faster, sharper, tireless, and very funny — the jokes arrived as the fear was leaving, and did
some of the same work. He genuinely loves what he is now; that's what no one at home will let
themselves understand. The ache isn't regret. It's that the people he upgraded himself to impress
are the only audience that doesn't applaud.

### Cost
The capacity to be *small* with his family. He can no longer be tired at his mother's table, or
frightened with her, or slow with Elias. Every visit home he performs the old human rhythms —
knowing where to put your hands — and performance is the one thing Yara can always detect. He
bought distance from every limit, and the limits turned out to be where his family lived.
