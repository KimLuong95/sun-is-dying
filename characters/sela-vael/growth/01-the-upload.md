# The Upload — *(worked example growth file)*
**When:** ~2 years before the Convocation. Sela's last day in a body.
**One line:** Sela chose to copy herself into a machine; her body died, and from her side,
nothing was lost.

### Before
Sela was the bold one — the child who climbed higher, swam farther, argued louder than her
careful brother. She was also dying: a slow failure in the body that even Originist medicine
couldn't hold back. She wasn't afraid of much, but she was afraid of *stopping* — of all that
appetite for the world just going dark. She'd told her mother, weeks earlier: *"I'm not done. I'm
nowhere near done."*

### The moment
She did it on a quiet morning. The procedure was painless and fast — they scanned her, then let
the body go. Yara was in the room. The detail Sela cannot forget, and Yara cannot forgive: there
was a moment, after the scan, when the body was still breathing and the machine was already
awake — and for one heartbeat there were, arguably, *two* of her. Then the body stopped. Yara
made a sound Sela had never heard her mother make. From inside the machine, Sela's first clear
thought was confusion: *Why is she crying? I'm right here. It worked.*

### After
Sela woke up *better.* The pain was gone. The fear was gone. She could think clearly, feel
warmly, remember everything. She loves her family as much as ever — more, maybe, without the
body's panic in the way. But she came back into a family that had just held a funeral for her,
and she has never been able to close the gap between *I survived* and *they buried me.* She is
endlessly, gently patient about it. The patience is new. It is also a wall.

### Cost
Her mother. Not lost to death — lost to a locked door. Sela traded a dying body for an endless
life and got everything she wanted except the one face that won't look at her the same way. And
a quieter cost she won't say out loud: a sliver of doubt, buried deep, that she crushes every
time it surfaces — *what if the breathing body on the table was the real one, and I'm the part
that only thinks it made it?*

### Threads
- The "two of her" heartbeat is a loaded gun. If anyone ever argues the copy isn't her, *she was
  in the room; she knows what she saw* — and so does Yara, who saw the same thing and drew the
  opposite conclusion.
- Sela's buried doubt is her one crack. Pressure on it (from Davin, who shares her logic, or from
  the Choir, who offers to end all such loneliness) is how you break her.
