# Vesha — and the Choir That Wears Her Face

**Branch:** The Choir · **Role:** Yara's sister, long gone into the union · **POV:** no — and
that's deliberate (we never get inside the "we")

## Snapshot
Before there was a wound between Yara and her children, there was Vesha — her older sister, who
joined the Choir when they were both young. For most of Yara's life "Vesha" has been a held
breath, a name that means *the first one I lost this way.* Now, for the Convocation, the Choir
has sent a body to speak — and it is Vesha's. Same face, aged strangely. Same crooked smile. And
behind the eyes, a thousand minds answering as one.

## How "she" appears
The Choir's representative looks like Yara's sister and remembers being her. It can laugh at an
old childhood joke, then in the same breath say *"we"* and mean a multitude. It reaches for Yara
with Vesha's hands and Vesha's tenderness — and it is asking, with that tenderness, for Yara to
stop being alone and *join.* The horror isn't menace. It's love offered by something that ate the
person who used to love you.

## How it talks
Warm, plural, and seamless. It slides between *"I remember"* and *"we believe"* without noticing
the seam — because to the Choir there is no seam. It speaks of loneliness the way someone speaks
of a disease they were cured of, and it cannot understand why Yara would refuse the cure. It is
never threatening. It is patient the way the tide is patient.

## The question it raises
When the Choir speaks with Vesha's voice — is that Vesha, still alive inside the union? A memory
the crowd keeps running? Or a mask the Choir wears because it knows Yara will listen to that face?
Yara doesn't know whether she's talking to her sister or to the thing that swallowed her. She
answers it anyway. She can't help it. It has Vesha's smile.

## Why it matters
Vesha is Yara's **oldest grief and her possible future** — proof of where this all leads if you
follow "never be alone again" to the end. And among all the branches pressing Yara to give up the
old human ways, the Choir presses hardest, because it presses with love, in a voice she has
missed her whole life.

## Arc seed (Book 1)
The Choir starts as a familiar comfort and becomes a quiet threat — not through violence, but
because its offer (join us, and the pain ends forever) grows more tempting exactly as everything
else falls apart. It may be the thing that nearly breaks Yara when she's weakest.
