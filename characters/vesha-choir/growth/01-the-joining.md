# The Joining — *(the first family fracture; told from outside, never from within)*
**When:** Yara and Vesha's young adulthood — decades before the children, before Davin.
**One line:** the heir to the keeping walked into the Choir, and the second daughter inherited
everything, including the grief.

### Before — *the part the family never says out loud*
**Vesha was the elder sister. The keeping was hers.** She was being raised to it the way Yara
never was — the laws, the languages, the Stone's cradle-rites — and she was good at all of it and
slept worse every year. Vesha was the loud one, the one who laughed with her whole mouth, and the
post was teaching her silence. What no one in the line had ever admitted: keeping is *lonely* the
way deep space is cold — uniformly, in every direction, forever. She did not break. She just kept
asking, in a house that had no answer: *why is the holiest work we have the one you must do
alone?*

Her name was Vera — the old word for *true*. Nobody used it. At home she was Vesha, the
kitchen-name, and home was the only place she lived until she didn't.

She called her little sister *little fish* — for how Yara, small, used to swim circles around the
garden cistern and never once asked to be let out.

### The moment
She didn't run away in the night. That would have been easier to forgive. She stood in the
kitchen and told them what the Choir had offered her — not power, not escape: **company, all the
way down** — and asked her family, the keepers of what humans were, to tell her honestly whether
staying human was worth never once being accompanied in her own head. No one answered well. Their
mother quoted law. Yara, too young and too frightened, said nothing at all — and has heard her
own silence every day since.

Vesha kissed the top of Yara's head and left before the lamps were lit. The Choir does not take;
it receives. That was the horror. Nothing was done to her. She *chose*, calmly, with the whole
family watching, against everything the family was.

### After
The line did what lines do: it closed the gap. Yara — the spare, the quiet one, *little fish* —
became the heir overnight, and was never once asked whether she wanted what her sister had set
down. Their mother said Vesha's name in lists of the dead. The first time the Choir sent a body
wearing Vesha's face to a Convocation, two keepers before Yara's time, the Custodian of that day
left the hall rather than be greeted by it.

### Cost
- **For Vesha:** everything and, by her own lights, nothing — there is no *her* left to bill.
  That unpayable, unfeelable cost is the Choir in one sentence.
- **For Yara:** her whole life. Every certainty she keeps was inherited from a sister who examined
  it and put it down. Yara's gripping was learned here: the first thing she ever held was a post
  someone had let go of.

### Threads
- *Little fish* (Ch.3, canon): the Choir kept the memory. Bait made of love — or proof Vesha
  persists. Never resolve cleanly (twist #6 / the flicker of her in Book 2).
- **The succession is a wound, not a fact.** When the branches ask Yara to surrender the Stone,
  they are asking the *replacement keeper* to fail where the real heir already walked away.
  Nobody in the hall knows how deep that cuts. The Choir does. It was there.
- The mother who "said Vesha's name in lists of the dead" — feeds the mother-shaped hole (ledger).
