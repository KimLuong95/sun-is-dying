# The Digitals

> *"I am not my body. I never was."*

## Who they are
The Digitals are people who **left their bodies behind**. They copied their minds — every
thought, memory, and habit — into machines, and now they live as pure thought inside vast
computers and worlds made of information. No flesh. No aging. No death by the body's failure.

## What they believe
To a Digital, **you are your mind, not your meat.** The body was only ever a vehicle — useful
once, like a first home you've long outgrown. Keep the mind running and *you* continue, exactly
as much "you" as you ever were. Death of the body, to them, is no more tragic than throwing away
worn-out shoes.

## How they live
Inside enormous computer-worlds they build for themselves — places that can look like anything
they imagine. They can run their thoughts fast or slow, pause themselves for a thousand years,
or make copies of themselves. Time and self are soft, bendable things to a Digital. This makes
them powerful, and a little hard for the other branches to even talk to.

## What they want for Earth
**Scan it — perfectly.** Record every stone, every plant, every buried memory, down to the
smallest detail, and keep that record safe forever. To a Digital, a perfect copy of Earth *is*
Earth. The dying lump of rock orbiting a dying sun is just the worn-out body. Save the pattern,
and you've saved the world.

## How the others see them
The Originists say the hardest thing of all: **a copy is not the person.** When you uploaded,
*you* died — your body went cold — and a recording that thinks it's you took your place. The
Digitals call that superstition. *I'm standing right here,* they say. *I remember everything. I
am more myself than ever.* No one has ever won this argument.

## Why they matter to the story
This is the **deepest family wound** in the book. When a mother's child uploads, the mother
buries a body — and then has to speak to something that wears her child's voice and insists it
*is* her child. Both of them are certain. Both of them are grieving. Neither can prove they're
right.
