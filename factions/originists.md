# The Originists

> *"We are the ones who stayed."*

## Who they are
The Originists are humans who chose to **stay human** — the same kind of body and mind we have
today. They are born, they grow, they age, and they die. They live a long time by our standards,
but they do not pretend to be anything more than what people have always been.

## What they believe
To an Originist, a human is the **original creature**: flesh, blood, and a single life lived in
a single body. They believe the others didn't *improve* on humanity — they walked away from it.
You can copy a mind, or rebuild a body, or join a thousand minds together, but the moment you
do, the human is gone and only an imitation is left wearing its name.

## How they live
On real, living worlds — planets with air and weather and soil. They tend these places like
gardens and treat birth and death as sacred, not as problems to be solved. Their lives are
short next to the other branches, and they think that shortness is part of what makes a life
mean something.

## What they want for Earth
**Save it as it is.** If the dying Sun makes Earth impossible, then carry its living things —
the seeds, the soil, the creatures — somewhere safe, intact and alive. Never let the cradle be
scanned into data, stripped for parts, or swallowed by the Choir. To an Originist, that wouldn't
be saving Earth. It would be the final insult to it.

## How the others see them
Stubborn. Sentimental. Doomed — fragile creatures clinging to weak bodies in a universe that is
busy killing them. The other branches don't always say it unkindly. Sometimes they say it the
way you'd speak of a relative who refuses to leave a house that's flooding.

## Why they matter to the story
**The Custodian family — House Vael — are Originists.** They keep Earth. So the branch that
believes most fiercely in *not changing* is the one holding the homeworld as it dies — and the
one being torn apart from the inside when its own children change anyway.
