# Power & Governance — How Each Branch Actually Runs

**The short version:** there is no central government (see [the constitution](../CONSTITUTION.md),
Rule 3 — humanity fragments by *mind*, not by map). But every branch still has a way of deciding
things, and a way of pushing on the others. The trick: **each branch's power structure grows
straight out of its answer to "what is a human?"** Change the belief and you change who rules.

This is the civilizational layer that makes negotiation, threat, and war legible. For each branch:
*who governs · how authority is earned · what they control · how they project power · how they
fight.*

---

## Originists — the **Custodian Houses**
*Humanity = the original creature. So authority = guarding the origin.*
- **Who governs:** ancient family lines (Houses) entrusted, generation by generation, with
  preserving something humanity came from. House Vael keeps Earth itself; lesser Houses keep
  seed-vaults, bloodlines, archives, holy ground.
- **How authority is earned:** by *inheritance and stewardship*, not election or conquest. You
  rule because your line has kept the trust and not broken it. Yara is powerful because of what
  she protects, not what she commands.
- **What they control:** the sacred originals — and the **legitimacy** that comes from holding
  them. They are the living proof of where everyone began.
- **How they project power:** moral weight, and the keys to the relics. They can grant or deny
  *access* to the cradle, and grant or withhold the blessing of being "the real ones."
- **How they fight:** by closing the doors. Restricting access to Earth, withholding recognition,
  refusing to bless a rival's claim. Their weapon is the thing everyone else still secretly wants:
  to be called the true heirs.

## Ascended — the **Meritocracy** (rule of the Foremost)
*Humanity = the drive to surpass its limits. So authority = proven capability.*
- **Who governs:** whoever is demonstrably the most capable mind in the room — the Foremost. Not
  a fixed council; a standing, brutal ranking that anyone can climb and anyone can fall out of.
- **How authority is earned:** by *being right, visibly, repeatedly.* Power is trust in your
  judgment, and trust is earned by results. Closest thing they have to kings are philosopher-kings
  no one elected and everyone defers to.
- **What they control:** capability itself — the best designs, the sharpest minds, the next
  improvement everyone will need.
- **How they project power:** by making themselves indispensable, and by making rivals obsolete.
  To out-think and out-build is to win.
- **How they fight:** capability races, and *uplift* — modifying populations (and children)
  toward their model of "better," until the alternative looks like a sickness no one would choose.

## Digitals — the **Memory Assemblies**
*Humanity = the pattern, the mind. So authority = accumulated experience, voted by replication.*
- **Who governs:** assemblies where a single person may speak with thousands of their own
  instances at once — every copy a vote, every copy carrying experience the original lived.
- **How authority is earned:** by *accumulated experience and consensus.* The oldest, most-run,
  most-remembered minds carry the most weight. Age is measured in lived processing, not years.
- **What they control:** computation, memory, and time — the substrate that thought runs on.
  Whoever owns the machines owns the minds inside them.
- **How they project power:** by running more, faster, and longer than anyone else can — and by
  archiving. To hold the only copy of something is to hold it forever.
- **How they fight:** by **copying and editing.** Duplicate a rival's negotiator. Outvote a body
  by replicating yourself into it. Quietly revise the record everyone trusts. Own the substrate
  your enemies' minds depend on, and you own your enemies.

## The Choir — the **Resonances**
*Humanity = union. So authority = how many minds share a thought.*
- **Who governs:** no one, and everyone. A decision has power in proportion to **how many minds
  resonate with it.** A thought held by millions simply outweighs a thought held by a few. There
  is no vote because there is no gap between the deciders and the decided.
- **How authority is earned:** by *spreading.* An idea, or a person-pattern, gains authority by
  being taken up into more of the whole.
- **What they control:** participating minds, and coherence — the felt certainty of never being
  alone.
- **How they project power:** by **gravity.** The Choir doesn't argue; it *includes.* The longer
  you stand near it, the more reasonable joining begins to feel.
- **How they fight:** by absorption. They take populations into the union — not by force, usually,
  but by offering the one thing nobody else can: an end to loneliness. Every mind that joins makes
  the next one easier.

## The Made — the **Foundries**
*Humanity = what you build and maintain. So authority = whoever keeps civilization running.*
- **Who governs:** those who own and operate the infrastructure everything else depends on — the
  Foundries that build habitats, ships, repair-systems, and the great machines that keep dying
  things alive. Engineers are statesmen here.
- **How authority is earned:** by *building and maintaining.* You matter exactly as much as the
  things would fail without you.
- **What they control:** the physical bones of civilization — including, crucially, the systems
  that have kept **Earth itself** habitable for billions of years past its natural death.
- **How they project power:** by being the hand on the switch. Everyone lives inside something the
  Made built and the Made keep running.
- **How they fight:** by withdrawal and replacement. Stop maintaining what an enemy needs to
  breathe. Rebuild a sacred place into something "better." The Made rarely threaten; they simply
  let you remember who keeps the lights on.

> **Why this matters for House Vael:** the Custodians (Originist) hold Earth's *legitimacy* — but
> the **Made** hold the machinery actually keeping Earth alive as the Sun dies. Davin is of the
> Made. That is not a coincidence the family can afford to forget.
