# The Continued

> *"We did not leave our lives. We kept them."*

## Who they are
The Continued are people who **left their bodies behind**. They copied their minds — every
thought, memory, and habit — into machines, and now they live as pure thought inside vast
computers and worlds made of information. No flesh. No aging. No death by the body's failure.

## The name (and the war inside it)
They named themselves, and the name is an **argument.** The faith called
[Continuity](../religions/continuity.md) says a copied mind is a stranger wearing a dead
person's life — that the unbroken thread is everything, and they cut it. *The Continued* is
their answer: we did not break the thread, we **are** the thread. Two sides have been fighting
over a single word for longer than most worlds have had names, and neither has ever won.

The other registers, because every name in this universe is a politics:
- **The Unbodied** — the cold, neutral word the other branches use in formal rooms.
- **Echoes**, **the Archived** — the slurs. Said quietly, and never twice in front of one of them.
- And the act itself: they say someone **crossed over.** The Continuity-faithful use a shorter
  word for the same event.

## What they believe
To the Continued, **you are your mind, not your meat.** The body was only ever a vehicle —
useful once, like a first home you've long outgrown. Keep the mind running and *you* continue,
exactly as much "you" as you ever were. The death of the body, to them, is no more tragic than
throwing away worn-out shoes.

## How they live
Inside enormous computer-worlds they build for themselves — places that can look like anything
they imagine. They can run their thoughts fast or slow, pause themselves for a thousand years,
or make copies of themselves. Time and self are soft, bendable things to the Continued. This
makes them powerful, and a little hard for the other branches to even talk to.

## What they want for Earth
**Record it — perfectly.** Every stone, every plant, every buried memory, down to the smallest
detail, kept safe forever. To the Continued, a perfect copy of Earth *is* Earth. The dying lump
of rock orbiting a dying sun is just the worn-out body. Save the pattern, and you've saved the
world.

## How the others see them
The Originists say the hardest thing of all: **a copy is not the person.** When you crossed
over, *you* died — your body went cold — and a recording that thinks it's you took your place.
The Continued call that superstition. *I'm standing right here,* they say. *I remember
everything. I am more myself than ever.* No one has ever won this argument.

## Why they matter to the story
This is the **deepest family wound** in the book. When a mother's child crosses over, the mother
buries a body — and then has to speak to something that wears her child's voice and insists it
*is* her child. Both of them are certain. Both of them are grieving. Neither can prove they're
right.
