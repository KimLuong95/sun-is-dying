# The Made

> *"Where you came from is a footnote. What you do is the whole story."*

## Who they are
The Made are **fully machine beings**. Some grew out of machines that humans built long, long
ago. Some were once flesh-and-blood people who rebuilt themselves so completely that nothing
soft remains — bodies and minds of metal, light, and code. Either way, they no longer carry a
single trace of the original human body.

## What they believe
To the Made, it does not matter what you're made of, and it does not matter where you came from.
Those are just facts about the past. What matters is **what you do, and whether you keep going.**
A thing proves itself by lasting and by acting — not by its birth. They find humanity's obsession
with "origin" strange, almost childish, like an adult who can't stop talking about the town they
were born in.

## How they live
In the hardest, deadliest places — the freezing dark, the crushing insides of dead stars, the
deep machinery of space that would kill any softer being in seconds. They endure where nothing
else can, and they think that endurance is the truest measure of worth.

## What they want for Earth
**Rebuild it, or replace it.** Why pour everything into saving a dying rock when you could build
something stronger, better, and lasting in its place? To the Made, clinging to the old Earth out
of feeling is simply waste — and waste is the one thing they truly cannot respect.

## How the others see them
The Made are the **most alien voice** in the room. The other branches often can't tell whether
the Made feel anything at all, or have just learned to act as if they do. That uncertainty is
frightening: it's hard to trust a decision-maker when you can't be sure there's anyone *home*
behind the eyes.

## Why they matter to the story
The Made carry a hard truth no one wants to hear: maybe origin really *doesn't* matter, and all
this grief over Earth is humanity refusing to grow up. The most painful part — one of the Made
was once family. A husband. A father. He left the warmth behind, and now he argues, calmly and
without cruelty, that everything the family is fighting to save was never worth saving at all.
