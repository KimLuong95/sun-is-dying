# Children of Sol

> *"She gave us everything. We will not look away while she dies."*

## What they believe
The Children of Sol hold that the **Sun is humanity's mother** — the literal source of all the
warmth, light, and life that ever made a human possible. Every cell that ever lived on Earth was
built from energy she gave. To worship anything else, they say, is to forget the breast that fed
you.

And now the mother is dying. To the Children of Sol this is not a disaster to flee. It is a
**sacred event** — the most important death in the history of the species, the passing of the one
thing all of humanity truly shares. How you meet that death is the deepest test of who you are.

## The three vigils
The faith is split, gently, into three ways of honoring the dying Sun — and these divisions
matter for the story:
- **The Witnesses** — believe humanity must *be present* for the end. To run away and not watch
  would be the ultimate betrayal of the mother. They will gather to see her die.
- **The Keepers** — believe the Sun must be *preserved* somehow — her death slowed, shelled, or
  her light captured and kept burning, so the mother is never truly gone.
- **The Faithful Dead** — believe the only honest thing is to **die with her.** A child does not
  outlive its mother by becoming a machine. When the Sun goes, they intend to go too.

## Why it's powerful
It takes the cold astronomy of a dying star and makes it the most intimate grief imaginable — the
death of a parent. Everyone understands that. And the three vigils give the faith a built-in
drama: even people who agree the Sun is sacred can come to blows over whether to *watch*,
*save*, or *join* her.

## How it shows up in the conflict
The Children of Sol turn the story's clock into an altar. As the end nears, their numbers swell
across every branch — even cold Made and plural Choir feel the old pull. The **Faithful Dead**
especially raise the stakes: a growing movement that doesn't *want* to be evacuated, that sees
survival itself as the betrayal. How do you save people who believe being saved is a sin?

## Relationship to the Cradle
The Children of Sol are the **sibling faith of [the Cradle](the-cradle.md).** The Cradle faces
the Earth — birthplace, lineage, the ground we came from. The Children of Sol face the Sun — the
star that made the ground possible. Many people hold both. Where they differ: the Cradle is about
*where we began*; the Children of Sol is about *how we should end*.
