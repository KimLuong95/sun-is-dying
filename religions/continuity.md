# Continuity

> *"A copy is not a person. Only the unbroken thread is you."*

## What they believe
Continuity is less a faith about the universe and more a faith about **the self.** Its one
article is simple and absolute: *you* are your **unbroken stream of consciousness** — the single,
never-interrupted thread of being awake, asleep, awake again, from birth to death. Break that
thread, even for an instant, and whatever wakes up afterward is **not you.** It only *thinks* it
is.

So to a Continuity believer, uploading isn't survival — it's death with a very convincing
impostor left behind. Rebuilding yourself as a machine, copying yourself, merging into the
Choir: all of it severs the thread. The new thing may have every memory, every habit, every
loved one's name. It is still a stranger wearing a dead person's life.

## Why it's powerful
It speaks to a fear everyone already has, deep down: *if you copied me perfectly and then shot
me, would I really live on — or would I just be dead, while a stranger enjoyed my life?* Continuity
says, plainly, **you'd be dead.** Many people across every branch feel the truth of that in their
gut, and build their whole identity around protecting the thread at all costs.

## Why it cuts across the branches
This is the most important thing about Continuity: **it isn't one branch's belief.** A flesh
Originist and a gene-modified Ascended can both be devout Continuity believers — both would
refuse to ever upload or copy, both would call a Digital a ghost. It even haunts the Digitals
themselves: some uploaded minds are tormented Continuity believers who suspect *they* are the
copy, and the real them died on the table.

## How it shows up in the conflict
Continuity is the philosophy that turns the upload question from science into **murder.** If the
Continuity believers are right, then half of what the other branches call "saving" people is
actually killing them and replacing them. That accusation — *you didn't evolve, you died and were
counterfeited* — is one of the deepest wounds at the Convocation, and it has no proof on either
side.

## Where it lives in the story
**This is the bedrock under [Yara and Sela](../characters/00-cast-and-relationships.md).** Yara
is, in her bones, a Continuity believer: she held her daughter's cooling body, so the bright,
loving thing on the screen *cannot* be Sela, no matter how much it remembers. And Sela's one
buried crack is a Continuity doubt she can't kill — *what if the body on the table was the real
me?* The whole mother-daughter tragedy is two people on opposite sides of this single belief.
