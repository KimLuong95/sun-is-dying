# The Cradle

> *"Forget where we're going. Remember where we began."*

## What they believe
The Cradle is the quietest faith, and in some ways the oldest. It doesn't argue much about
creators or accidents or future universes. It holds one thing sacred: **Earth itself.** The one
place life is known to have ever begun. The single point in all of creation that every living
human can trace themselves back to.

To a follower of the Cradle, that fact is holy on its own. You don't need a creator to make a
birthplace sacred. Earth is where we *started* — and a people who forget where they started, they
say, stop being a people at all. The clue in the universe, the empty sky, the dying Sun: none of
it changes the one duty that matters. **Protect where we came from.**

## Why it's powerful
It's the faith of memory and belonging, and it speaks straight to the heart. Everyone, no matter
how strange they've become, came from somewhere — and the Cradle gives that feeling a home. It's
also the faith most tied to *family*, to lineage, to the long chain of ancestors reaching back
to one blue world.

## How it shows up in the conflict
This is the faith of the **Custodians** and the Originists — but not only them. You'll find
quiet Cradle believers in every branch, even among the Made and the Choir: people who changed
everything about themselves but could never quite let go of the homeworld. When Earth dies, the
Cradle faithful across all five branches feel it as a death in the family — which makes them
unpredictable allies and dangerous when grieving.

## The shadow side
Love of the past can curdle into a refusal to let anything move forward. The most rigid Cradle
believers would rather see humanity die *with* Earth, honestly and whole, than survive by
becoming something Earth's first children wouldn't recognize. Devotion and a death wish can look
remarkably alike.
