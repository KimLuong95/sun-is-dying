# The Next Cause

> *"If a universe can make minds, then minds can make a universe. That is the whole point of us."*

## What they believe
The Next Cause takes the clue and reads it as a **destiny, not a comfort.** Yes, they say, the
universe was made on purpose — but not so we could sit and worship the one who made it. We were
made to *take the next turn.* A universe gave birth to minds; now those minds are meant to give
birth to the next universe. Creation is supposed to pass down the line, parent to child, forever.

To them, the dying Sun and the dying Earth are not a tragedy at all. They are a **birth.** An
ending that makes a beginning possible. The whole sorrow of the age — the cold stars, the empty
sky, the last cradle going dark — is just the labor pains of something new.

## Why it's powerful
It turns the most depressing facts in the universe into the most hopeful. Everything is dying?
Good — that's how room is made. We're alone? Of course we are; **we haven't been born yet, as
makers.** No other belief gives people a reason to feel *excited* standing at the end of all
things. That excitement is intoxicating, and a little frightening.

## How it shows up in the conflict
The Next Cause doesn't really care who inherits Earth — it cares who will inherit *creation
itself*. Its followers push humanity toward enormous, reckless ambition: projects to reshape
reality, to reach the very bottom of how the universe is built and learn to light a new one. To
everyone else, this can look like madness dressed as faith.

## The shadow side
People who believe they are mid-wifing the next universe can justify almost anything in the
present one. What's one dying world, one lost branch, one war, weighed against the birth of
*everything that comes next?* The Next Cause is the belief most willing to sacrifice the now for
the someday — and that makes it the most quietly ruthless faith of all.
