# The Real Catastrophe — What Actually Goes Wrong

> The hardest question, asked plainly: **what happens if no one agrees?** A clock and a debate
> aren't enough. This file defines the *disaster* that turns a meeting into a war and makes the
> reader say "I have to know what happens next."

So far we had: the Sun's death = the **clock**, Earth's fate = the **conflict**, identity = the
**theme**. Missing: the **catastrophe**. Here it is, in four tightening layers.

## Layer 1 — The clock just broke
For billions of years, the Sun's death was a distant certainty — something for the
great-great-grandchildren to handle. **It just stopped being distant.** New readings show the
Sun has lurched into a rapid, unpredicted phase. The window everyone planned around — centuries —
has collapsed to a **handful of years.** The Convocation that was supposed to be a slow,
ceremonial farewell is now an emergency, and no one is ready.

## Layer 2 — Every fix cancels the others, and the first move is final
The five branches don't just *disagree* about Earth — their five plans are **physically
mutually exclusive and irreversible.** You cannot scan Earth into data *and* keep the living
biosphere *and* shell the planet to outlast the Sun *and* let the Faithful Dead go down with it.
The first faction to **act** erases everyone else's option forever.

That's the trap that makes war: it's no longer enough to win the argument. You have to **act
first** — and stop the others from acting first. A debate where the loser loses *permanently,
the instant someone moves,* is not a debate. It's a standoff with the safeties off.

## Layer 3 — Earth is the last window onto why anything exists
This is the layer that lifts it from sad to enormous. Tie it to the one cosmic mystery
([the Big-Bang clue](../world/01-when-and-where.md)):

**The origin-anomaly can only be *read* from a truly unbroken, natural frame** — a place reality
has been left untouched. Across the whole galaxy, after five billion years of humanity reshaping
everything it touched, **Earth/Sol is the last such place** — kept pristine, uselessly, lovingly,
by the Custodians. When the Sun dies, that window closes. **Forever.**

So Earth isn't just the cradle of *life*. It may be humanity's one and only chance to answer the
question every religion is built on: *were we made on purpose, or are we an accident?* Every
faith's entire claim — the Makers, the Silence, the Next Cause — rides on an answer that dies
with the Sun.

> **Why this doesn't break the constitution (Rule 5).** This is *not* "whoever grabs the Earth-
> object wins." Reading the answer requires *being unbroken* — staying close to the original. So
> the prize is tied to **identity itself**: the fight over who can read the universe's answer
> *is* the fight over who is the truest human. The branches that "improved" themselves may have
> blinded themselves to the one thing that mattered. The McGuffin is *what you've become.*

## Layer 4 — Doing nothing is the worst outcome of all
Put it together and inaction is suicide. If no one decides before the clock runs out, the Sun
takes **everything at once**: the cradle, all original Earthly life, and the only window onto the
origin-answer. Total, permanent loss.

So they *must* act — but **any** action betrays four other futures, and almost certainly someone
they love. **Move and you betray your family. Wait and you lose everything.** That is the
catastrophe.

## The stakes ladder (now with teeth)
- **Personal:** Yara will have to choose between saving Earth and holding her family — the two
  things she's built her life on are now opposites.
- **Civilizational:** the first irreversible act starts the war the constitution promised for
  later. Book 1 lights the fuse; it doesn't yet explode.
- **Cosmic:** the answer to *why anything exists* is on a timer, and the timer is the dying Sun.
