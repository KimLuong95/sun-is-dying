# Book 1 — Outline (v2)

> **Working title:** *The First Stone* (alts: *The Last Table*, *The Custodian*)
>
> **v2, revised after Ch.1–4 were drafted.** The manuscript invented something the v1 outline
> didn't have — the First Stone — and it turned out to be the spine object of the whole series.
> This revision absorbs it. The rule stands: **does Yara's story work?** The Convocation is the
> stage; Yara is the play.

---

## Yara — the engine

**What she WANTS (the goal she'll chase):**
To save Earth as it is, *and* keep her family from scattering into things she can't recognize.
Hold the cradle. Hold the children. Lose nothing.

**What she NEEDS (the truth she's avoiding):**
To learn that *holding on* and *loving* are not the same thing — that being a good Custodian, and
a good mother, may mean opening her hands. To grieve honestly what's already gone instead of
refusing the grief, and to love what is actually in front of her rather than the version she's
clutching.

**The lie she believes:**
*"If I just hold tightly enough, nothing will truly be lost — and any loss means I failed my
duty."*

**Why want and need are opposites (this is the whole book):**
The catastrophe makes saving Earth and holding her family **mutually exclusive**. Every plan that
might save the cradle costs her a child; every way to keep her family means letting the cradle
die. And the Stone makes it physical: the lie ("hold tightly enough and nothing is lost") is now
literally in her arms, in front of everyone, and holding it *is the act of war.* Her *want* is
impossible. Her *need* — choose, and accept the loss — is the one thing her identity is built to
refuse.

**The arc in four words:** *Gripping → cracking → choosing → open hands.*

## The dramatic question of Book 1
> **When you can't save everything, what do you choose to be — the keeper of what was, or the
> mother of what's left?**

## The Stone ↔ the mirror (series design intent)
The two great objects of the series are the same question wearing two faces:
- **The First Stone** asks: *who is still continuous with the first hand?* (the past's question —
  heirship)
- **The anomaly** asks: *can anything unbroken still put the question to the universe?* (the
  future's question — origin)

Both are about **what is still unbroken.** Design intent: at the Book 1 climax they begin to
converge — the legitimacy fight and the origin window turn out to be one problem — and the series
runs on that convergence. Mechanics stay undefined until drafting demands them. **Never explain
the rhyme on the page; let readers find it.** (Rule 7 governs: the mirror never confirms anyone.)

## The beat sheet
*Close third person, Yara's POV as the spine (other POVs used sparingly). Intimate. The war is
only being **born** here — see [escalation](../CONSTITUTION.md#the-central-conflict-escalating-across-the-series).*

### ✅ ON THE PAGE — Ch.1–4 (Act I, first half)
1. **The Chairs** — the table, the plate for Sela, the Stone seeded as nameless dread, the early
   red dusk. *(Opening scene as designed, plus the Stone plant v1 didn't have.)*
2. **The Man Who Knocked** — Davin at dawn; the clock broken (years, not centuries); the lattice
   claimed ("they're ours"); *leave tonight*; "the world is already lost."
3. **The First Stone** — the Convocation staged as conclave/funeral/succession. The Stone
   introduced; four claims on its meaning; the Foremost's provocation ("We should lift it");
   Yara lifts it.
4. **Politely, At First** — the lift reads as a *claim*; the Made answer with the lattice flicker
   ("this world lives because we permit it"); the darkness witnessed; the race is now real.

> **Where this leaves the design:** the v1 "Act I turn" (safeties off, someone will move first)
> has already happened by Ch.4 — earlier than planned, which is good. What v1 lacked: Yara has
> been **reactive** for four chapters (counting, receiving, carrying, reflex-gripping). From Ch.5
> she must start **acting** — making moves that cost her something on purpose.

### ▶ NEXT — Act I completion map (Ch.5–9)
5. **The Guests.** Recess; nobody leaves orbit. Each power comes to the house *privately* — not
   threats, courtship: the Foremost (settle it before someone settles it for you), the nine-bodied
   delegate (let us hold a perfect record of it), the Choir (bring it where nothing is ever lost),
   Davin (silence; he already said it). Each visit dramatizes one power structure. A real beat
   with Ren — and the number he checks and tells no one about. **Ends:** the Faithful Dead arrive —
   pilgrim barges visible from the garden, people coming to die with the Sun. Scale that is human,
   not machine.
6. **Sela.** The postponed conversation — the book's heart, untouched since Ch.1, comes due. And
   the **first anomaly whisper** arrives through her: in the deep scans of the dying Sun there is
   something the instruments keep refusing to file as noise. She tells her mother before she tells
   her Assembly — because the one thing she cannot copy is being believed by her mother.
7. **Another One.** Yara asks Elias what he meant. The previous convulsion; what the keepers
   before her knew; the first hint that "as it was" may not mean what she thinks *(seed twist #1)*;
   the mother-shaped hole in the family story *(see threads ledger)*. His age sharpened as
   mystery, never answered.
8. **The First Move.** The Made announce lattice triage — the new solar schedule cannot be held
   everywhere, so shade must be withdrawn from something: the wild lands or the settlements, the
   garden or the graves. Not a threat; arithmetic. Refusal-has-consequences becomes *policy*, and
   every faction's "rescue" plan suddenly has a deadline attached.
9. **ACT I OUT — the Keeper moves.** Yara stops reacting. She uses the only power she has — the
   keeper's legitimacy and the Stone itself — to make her own irreversible move (working
   candidate: she invokes the choosing *on her own terms and timetable*, making herself the clock
   instead of the prize — or takes the Stone out of the hall entirely, daring the law to follow
   her home). Whatever the form: **she acts, it cannot be taken back, and it costs her a family
   member's trust on the spot.** Act II begins.

### ★ MIDPOINT — the daughter sees God *(twist #3 + the cosmic stake lands)*
Sela — following the whisper from Ch.6 — **confirms the link**: Earth is the last unbroken place
from which the origin-anomaly can be read, and the dying Sun will close that window forever. The
fight over a sentimental rock becomes the fight over *the last chance to ask why anything exists*
(the anomaly never answers cleanly — it's a mirror; Rule 7). And the only person who could find it
was the child Yara buried. **Yara has to listen to "the copy" to understand the most important
thing in history** — her certainty takes its first real crack. The board flips: every faction is
desperate now, and Yara needs Sela.

### ACT II — pulled apart *(both loves, both philosophies, escalate)*
Yara caught between cradle and family while the cosmic stake makes every side righteous and
frantic. She lies to one to protect another. Elias's hints deepen *(twists #1, #4)*. The Faithful
Dead swell from pilgrimage into movement — how do you save people who believe being saved is a
sin? Trust rots. The Stone, wherever Yara has put it, warps every conversation around itself.

### ACT II TURN — someone moves *(the low point)*
A family member acts irreversibly to seize Earth's fate — someone Yara loves (strong candidate:
**Davin**, calmly, "to end everyone's suffering"; or **Sela**, beginning the scan her Assembly
demands). The first true shot of the war. Yara moves to stop them — and in doing so **betrays
someone, and someone is lost** (protect the choice; the death must cost the reader). Gripping has
failed her completely.

### ★ CLIMAX — *to look, the cradle must burn* (twist #8, fused with the Stone)
The Sun's first lethal convulsion arrives at the deadline. The shape of the choice is now bare:
**the only way to look into the mirror — humanity's one chance to ask why it exists — consumes
the living Earth in the act.** Keep the cradle and stay blind forever, or burn it to look, and
still not be sure what you saw. Yara — Custodian, keeper, the woman whose whole life is *not
letting go* — holds both the Stone and the choice. She chooses, not cleanly, at a price that pays
off her **need**: open hands. *(Exact mechanism decided in drafting; the Stone must be physically
present in the scene — what she does with it IS the choice made visible.)*

### ★ FINAL SCENE — the answer answers back
Aftermath, not tidy. Earth's fate set in motion, not resolved (that's the series). The family
changed, scattered, bound by a fresh wound. Then the hook: **the anomaly *responds*** — the
signature shifts in the wake of what they did, as if something noticed *(first flicker of #10,
never confirmed).* For the first time in five billion years, the universe may have answered. No
one knows what it said. **End on that.**

## What lands in Book 1 vs. later
- **Committed for Book 1:** the catastrophe (all 4 layers), twist **#3** (Sela finds the link),
  twist **#8** (to look, the cradle must burn), the Stone as the physical spine.
- **Seeded in Book 1, paid later:** **#1** (the cradle is a restoration), **#4** (humanity
  shattered over this once before), the first flicker of **#10** (the mirror stirs), "We remember
  dark" (the Choir can fear), Ren's number, the mother-shaped hole.
- **Held for Books 2+:** #2, #5, #6, #7, #9 and the reserve twists.

## The test (run on every chapter)
1. In every scene, what does Yara **want**, and what does it **cost**? If unclear, fix the scene.
2. From Ch.5 on: is Yara **acting** or only reacting? Two reactive chapters in a row = fix it.
3. Does the chapter end on a hook, a turn, or a dropped floor — never a tidy bow?
4. Is the big idea carried by a **person** in the room? (Rule 6.)
