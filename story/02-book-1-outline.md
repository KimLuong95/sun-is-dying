# Book 1 — Outline

> **Working title:** *The Last Table* (alt: *Who Inherits the Dawn*, *The Custodian*)
>
> The whole universe is a test of one thing: **does Yara's story work?** If we feel her, the
> world is real. If we don't, no amount of lore saves it. So this outline is built around her,
> not around the politics. The Convocation is the stage; Yara is the play.

---

## Yara — the engine

**What she WANTS (the goal she'll chase):**
To save Earth as it is, *and* keep her family from scattering into things she can't recognize.
Hold the cradle. Hold the children. Lose nothing.

**What she NEEDS (the truth she's avoiding):**
To learn that *holding on* and *loving* are not the same thing — that being a good Custodian, and
a good mother, may mean opening her hands. To grieve honestly what's already gone instead of
refusing the grief, and to love what is actually in front of her rather than the version she's
clutching.

**The lie she believes:**
*"If I just hold tightly enough, nothing will truly be lost — and any loss means I failed my
duty."*

**Why want and need are opposites (this is the whole book):**
The catastrophe makes saving Earth and holding her family **mutually exclusive**. Every plan that
might save the cradle costs her a child; every way to keep her family means letting the cradle
die. Her *want* (save everything) is now impossible. Her *need* (choose, and accept the loss)
is the only way through — and it's the one thing her whole identity is built to refuse.

**The arc in four words:** *Gripping → cracking → choosing → open hands.*

## The dramatic question of Book 1
> **When you can't save everything, what do you choose to be — the keeper of what was, or the
> mother of what's left?**

## The beat sheet
*Close third person, Yara's POV as the spine (other POVs used sparingly). Intimate. The war is
only being **born** here — see [escalation](../CONSTITUTION.md#the-central-conflict-escalating-across-the-series).*

### ★ OPENING SCENE — *the chairs*
Yara, alone on a quiet Earth, prepares the old family house for a reunion she dreads — airing
rooms, carrying chairs to the long table. She stops, counting, because she doesn't know how to
set it. Does a daughter who uploaded need a chair? Does the Choir get one seat or many? Does a
husband made of metal sit? The chair-counting *is* the book: **how do you set a family table
when you no longer know what your family is?** Then the light through the window is wrong — too
red, too soon. The message comes: the Sun's reading has changed; the years they thought they had
are gone. She sets the chair down. *(Show the theme; never name it. See [style](../style/01-avoiding-ai-tells.md).)*

### INCITING INCIDENT — the emergency Convocation
The accelerated death + the summons. A ceremonial farewell becomes a countdown. Her scattered
family — and the branches they speak for — are coming home to Earth to decide its fate before the
clock runs out.

### ACT I — the arrivals *(the reunions that break your heart)*
Each return dramatizes a branch through a *relationship*, not a lecture:
- **Sela** (uploaded) — the unbearable one. Yara can't say her name to her face.
- **Ren** (Ascended) — charm, speed, and the ache for a mother's approval he'll never quite get.
- **Davin** (the Made) — calm, courteous, devastating; the love that cooled instead of breaking.
- **The Choir, wearing Vesha** — Yara's lost sister, smiling, saying "we."
- **Elias** is already there. He's always there. He won't take a side.
The five positions on Earth's fate are laid out — and revealed to be **mutually exclusive.**

### ACT I TURN — the safeties come off
The discovery that the five plans cancel each other *and that someone is already preparing to act
first.* The debate stops being safe. Whoever moves first wins reality. The room changes
temperature.

### ★ MIDPOINT — the daughter sees God *(twist #3 + the cosmic stake lands)*
Sela — scanning Earth for her own faction's plan — **discovers the link**: Earth is the last
unbroken place from which the origin-anomaly can be read, and the dying Sun will close that
window forever. The fight over a sentimental rock becomes the fight over *the last chance to ask
why anything exists* (the anomaly never answers cleanly — it's a mirror; see Rule 7). And the only
person who could find it was the child Yara buried. **Yara has to
listen to "the copy" to understand the most important thing in history** — her certainty takes its
first real crack. The board flips: now every faction is desperate, and Yara needs Sela.

### ACT II — pulled apart *(both loves, both philosophies, escalate)*
Yara is caught between saving the cradle and holding her family, and the cosmic stake makes both
sides righteous and frantic. She lies to one to protect another. Elias hints at something that
unsteadies her further *(seed twist #1: the cradle may already be a restoration; seed twist #4:
humanity has faced a reckoning like this once before — and shattered).* The Children of Sol's
**Faithful Dead** swell — people who refuse to be saved at all. Trust rots.

### ACT II TURN — someone moves *(the low point)*
A family member acts irreversibly to seize Earth's fate — and it's someone Yara loves (strong
candidate: **Davin**, calmly, "to end everyone's suffering," begins his plan; or **Sela** starts
the scan). The first irreversible act is the first shot of the war. Yara must move to stop them —
and in doing so **betrays someone, and someone is lost** (protect the choice; a death here should
cost the reader). Her family fractures for real. Gripping has failed her completely.

### ★ CLIMAX — *to hear it, the cradle must die* (twist #8)
All threads converge as the Sun hits its first lethal convulsion at the deadline. The terrible
shape of the choice is now clear: **the only way to look into the mirror — humanity's one chance
to ask why it exists — consumes the living Earth in the act.** Keep the cradle and stay blind
forever, or burn the cradle to look (and still not be sure what you saw). Yara, the Custodian sworn to preserve Earth above all, holds the choice
in her hands. She cannot save everything. She chooses — *not cleanly, and at a cost that pays off
her **need***: she lets go of the relic to protect the living future (her family, the answer),
or refuses all five plans and finds a third path that costs her the Custodian identity itself.
Whatever the exact mechanism, the climax forces **want vs. need** and she finally chooses *need.*

### ★ FINAL SCENE — the answer answers back
Aftermath, not tidy. Earth's fate is set in motion (not fully resolved — that's the series). The
family is changed, scattered, bound by a fresh wound. Then the hook: **the anomaly *responds*** —
the signature shifts in the wake of what they did, as if something noticed *(seed of twist #3's
big sibling, #10).* For the first time in 5 billion years, the universe may have *answered.* And
no one knows what it said. **End on that.**

## What lands in Book 1 vs. later
- **Committed for Book 1:** the catastrophe (all 4 layers), twist **#3** (Sela discovers the
  link), twist **#8** (cradle must die to be heard).
- **Seeded in Book 1, paid later:** **#1** (cradle is a restoration), **#4** (humanity shattered
  over this once before), the first flicker of **#10** (the mirror seems to stir).
- **Held for Books 2+:** #2, #5, #6, #7, #9 and the reserve twists.

## The test (run before drafting Chapter 1)
1. In every scene, what does Yara **want**, and what does it **cost**? If unclear, fix the scene.
2. Does the chapter end on a hook, a turn, or a dropped floor — never a tidy bow?
3. Is the big idea carried by a **person** in the room? (Rule 6.)
