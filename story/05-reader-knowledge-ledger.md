# Reader-Knowledge Ledger — What a First-Time Reader Actually Knows

> The writer knows everything; the reader knows only what the page gave them. This ledger tracks
> the gap. **Update after every chapter draft.** An item only counts as KNOWN if it's stated
> plainly somewhere a tired phone-reader will catch it — an epigraph, a clear line of dialogue, a
> plain narration sentence. Implication alone does not count.

Format per chapter: **KNOWS** (new plain facts) · **WONDERS** (open hooks) · **RISK** (what a
reader might misread — and the fix).

---

## Ch. 1 — The Chairs
**KNOWS:** one world (Earth), one original people that became many kinds; the star is old; one
family stays to keep the world *(primer epigraph)*. Yara's daughter Sela exists as a machine-body
copy of a dead girl — and insists she's alive. An uncountably old man (Elias) lives with Yara.
Something under the house must be carried out tomorrow. The dusk came an hour early — and even
Elias finds that ominous.
**WONDERS:** is Sela her daughter or a copy? What's under the house? What is "another one"?
**RISK (found in audit):** ~~reader can't tell this is the far future / that the premise is "many
kinds of human"~~ → fixed by the primer epigraph. ~~"For the talking" unclear~~ → acceptable; Ch.2
names the Convocation.

## Ch. 2 — The Man Who Knocked
**KNOWS:** the timeline collapsed — centuries became a few years *(notice epigraph + "Not
centuries, Yara. Years")*. Davin is her estranged husband, now a machine-person. A "Convocation"
is gathering to decide Earth. The sky is artificial — the lattice — and it belongs to Davin's
people. Whoever acts first decides everything.
**WONDERS:** will she run? What are the factions actually like?
**RISK:** ~~"Convocation" used but undefined~~ → the notice epigraph anchors it; Ch.3's writ
defines it fully.

## Ch. 3 — The First Stone
**KNOWS:** there are **five kinds** of humanity, named and listed *(writ epigraph)*. The First
Stone = the oldest human-made thing; only "the heir of all humanity" may lift it; there has never
been one. The five kinds each read the Stone as proof of *their* claim. Yara, the keeper, lifted
it.
**WONDERS:** what happens when a keeper lifts it? Is Vesha still inside the Choir-thing?
**RISK (found in audit):** ~~the five-branch taxonomy was never countable on the page~~ → fixed
by the writ epigraph naming all five in plain words.

## Ch. 4 — Politely, At First
**KNOWS:** the keeper's law is lift → offer → choose, "and no fourth" *(law epigraph)* — so
Yara holding on is a violation everyone can read. Holding = claiming. The Made answered by
switching off Earth's sky for a heartbeat: the world lives because they permit it. The whole
galaxy watched; the race is now real.
**WONDERS:** what will the other branches do about her claim? Was the flicker an order or a rogue
hand?
**RISK:** the lattice arriving from nowhere → already fixed in the coherence pass (seeded Ch.1–2).

## Ch. 5 — The Guests
**KNOWS:** whoever holds the Stone speaks for the species — which is why everyone courts the
holder *(records epigraph)*. Each branch's power has a shape: the Foremost's reason, the
Continued's perfect record, the Made's maintenance, the Choir's company. A hundred thousand
pilgrims (the Faithful Dead) have landed to die with the Sun — a sixth claim: let it end.
**WONDERS:** what is Ren hiding (the wrist)? Why has no Sela come back? What will Yara *do*?
**RISK:** five visits could blur together → mitigated by hard variation (lengths, registers, one
unopened door).

## Ch. 6 — Your Name in Another Room
**KNOWS:** the Pattern exists — an ancient, contested "looks meant" signature under reality;
every faith is built on reading it; no one ever has *(primer epigraph)*. Sela found that from
Earth it reads differently ("your name in another room") and told her mother before her own
people — because belief can't be copied. If it gets out, Earth becomes an instrument. Yara said
her daughter's name for the first time since the funeral.
**WONDERS:** what does "from here" mean? What will Yara do with the most dangerous fact alive?
What does "I didn't tell the rest of me" mean for the other Selas?

## Ch. 7 — Another One
**KNOWS:** the Sun lurched once before, long ago; afterwards the garden *died and was regrown* —
Earth's "unbroken" nature is (per Elias) a restoration, and the keepers cut three seasons from
their own records to hide it. The proverb that settled "is a regrown thing still itself" has
lost its ending. Yara's mother knew — "Forgive us. It worked," in her hand, in the margins.
**WONDERS:** is Elias right or fogged? What was cut? Which way did the proverb end? (Never
answered.) What else did the mother know?

## Ch. 8 — Shade Is a Service
**KNOWS:** the lattice can no longer shade everything; the Foundries now choose, hour by hour,
what stands in the sun *(notice epigraph)*. Every branch's "relief" is recruitment — exits, not
shelter. Davin locked Yara/house/garden as unpetitionable protection long ago and refuses to
remove it. The pilgrims formally petitioned to be unshaded first — the first request in history
to ask for the sun.
**WONDERS:** what stands in the sun? What will the petition force?

## Ch. 9 — The Binding
**KNOWS:** Yara acted: the choosing WILL happen — at Earth's final sunset, not before; until
then the world is sealed (no scan, shell, harvest, song), and touching it forfeits the Stone
forever. All five kinds assented — too smoothly. The unsigned hand (mother's) taught the move:
"a keeper who only keeps will lose everything" *(epigraph)*.
**WONDERS:** what does racing-in-the-dark look like? Elias: "That was true the last time, too" —
what last time?

## Ch. 10 — The Miracle
**KNOWS:** Ren is dying — divergence, the ascension failing; he refuses every exit; that's why
he came home. And overnight in the camps, the dying stopped dying — someone "repaired" a hundred
thousand people who came to die, unasked; no kind claims it; the pilgrims call it *theft*. It
exploits the Binding's loophole: people aren't the world.
**WONDERS:** who did it? Can Ren be saved in a way he'd accept? What will the camps do?
**RISK:** the miracle must not read as supernatural → Ren's law-reading line anchors it as a
deliberate, skilled act by an unknown faction.

## Standing audit notes
- **The premise is now stated, plainly, once, early** (Ch.1 primer). Never assume a reader
  carries the back-cover blurb in their head.
- **Counting matters.** "Five kinds," "three steps," "one family" — plain numbers are handrails.
  Use them in epigraphs and dialogue freely; they cost nothing and orient everything.
- **Each chapter's first half-page must answer:** where are we, when is this relative to the last
  chapter, who is in the scene. In motion is good; in motion *and* oriented is the standard.
