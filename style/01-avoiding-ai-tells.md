# Avoiding AI Tells — The Anti-Machine Kit

> Readers can smell AI prose, and they hate it. This file is the defense. It's blunt on purpose.
> Most of these are habits *I* (the AI) fall into by default, so they get hunted on every pass.

The core problem with AI writing isn't grammar — it's that it's **smooth, balanced, tidy, and
emotionally over-explained.** Real writing is lumpier, braver, and trusts the reader more. Fix
the tells below and 80% of the "machine" feeling disappears.

---

## A. Banned and rationed phrases
**Banned outright** (delete on sight):
- "a testament to," "stands as a testament," "a symphony of," "a tapestry of," "a dance of"
- "in a world where…", "little did they know," "only time would tell"
- "navigate the complexities of," "delve into," "a stark reminder"
- "it was a mix of [emotion] and [emotion]"
- "a shiver ran down [their] spine," "let out a breath [they] didn't know [they] were holding"
- "the weight of it all," "the gravity of the situation"

**Rationed** (max once per chapter, and only if truly earned):
- "somehow," "something," "a kind of," "almost," "as if" — hedging words. AI hides behind them.
  Commit to the concrete thing instead.

## B. The "not just X, but Y" disease
AI loves the shape: *"It wasn't just a planet, it was a promise." / "This was more than grief —
it was…"* It feels profound and is almost always hollow.
- **Rule:** no more than **one** "not just… but…" construction per chapter, if any.
- **Fix:** state the real thing plainly, once. *"It was a promise."* Trust it.

## C. The rule of three
AI instinctively writes in triads: *"cold, empty, and silent." "She had loved him, fought him,
buried him."* Three beats feels complete — which is exactly why it gets numbing.
- **Rule:** break the pattern. Use two. Use four. Use one and stop.
- A list of two with a hard period hits harder than a tidy three.

## D. Stop explaining the emotion you just showed
The #1 AI tell. It shows a thing, then *tells you what to feel about it.*
- ❌ *She set down the cup without drinking. The gesture revealed how deeply the loss had shaken
  her, a grief she could barely contain.*
- ✅ *She set down the cup without drinking.*
- **Rule:** if you've shown it, **delete the sentence that explains it.** The reader is not
  stupid. Let them do the work — that's what makes them feel something.

## E. Don't name the theme
AI states the moral out loud so nobody misses it.
- ❌ *And in that moment she understood that holding on too tightly was its own kind of losing.*
- ✅ *(just show her grip the railing until her knuckles whiten, and end the scene)*
- **Rule:** the theme lives in `/world` and `/characters`. It must **never** be spoken by the
  narrator or, except very rarely, by a character. Themes are felt, not announced.

## F. Em-dash and rhythm tics
- AI overuses the em-dash (—) for false drama. Cap it: roughly **one per page**, and prefer a
  period.
- AI writes sentences of similar length, which lulls. **Vary hard.** A long, winding sentence,
  then three words. Then one. Fragments are allowed. Real voice has a pulse.
- AI starts too many sentences with "As," "With," "There was," and participles ("Walking in,
  she…"). Hunt and break these.

## G. Over-described faces and bodies
AI fills space with smiles, eyes, breaths, and nods that mean nothing.
- ❌ *He smiled softly, his eyes crinkling, and let out a small breath as he nodded.*
- ✅ cut it, or replace with **one** specific, character-revealing action.
- **Rule:** a gesture earns its place only if it tells us something true about *that* person.
  Generic body language is filler.

## H. False balance and tidiness
AI resolves everything and wraps scenes in a bow.
- Leave threads loose. Let an argument end without a winner. End a scene one beat too early.
- Not every chapter needs a neat closing line. The best endings often feel like the floor
  dropped out.

## I. Vague grandeur
AI reaches for cosmic-sounding nothing: *"the vast tapestry of existence," "an ancient, knowing
presence," "the infinite expanse."*
- **Rule:** replace abstraction with the **specific and small.** Not "the weight of five billion
  years" — *the dust on a chair no one has sat in since before there were words for chairs.*
- Concrete beats cosmic. Always.

## J. Dialogue that's too articulate
AI characters speak in complete, balanced, self-aware paragraphs. People don't.
- People interrupt, dodge, understate, change the subject, and fail to say the thing.
- See [`03-dialogue.md`](03-dialogue.md) for the full treatment.

---

## The five-second sniff test
Before any passage is "done," read it and ask:
1. Did I explain a feeling I already showed? → **cut the explanation.**
2. Is there a tidy triad or a "not just… but…"? → **break it.**
3. Did the narrator say what it all means? → **delete that.**
4. Are all my sentences the same length? → **smash the rhythm.**
5. Is anything here vague-profound instead of specific? → **make it small and real.**

If all five are clean, it probably doesn't smell like a machine anymore.
