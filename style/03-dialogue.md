# Dialogue — How These People Talk

Dialogue is where AI writing gets caught fastest, because real people are *bad at talking* in
ways that are very specific. Here's how to make it live.

## The golden rule: people don't say what they mean
Real conversation is mostly **dodging, hinting, and missing.** Characters want something and are
afraid to ask for it directly, so they talk *around* it.
- Yara, breaking inside, asks *"Have you eaten?"* instead of *"I can't lose you too."*
- The thing left unsaid should be louder than the thing said.
- **Rule:** before writing a line, know what the character *actually* wants — then have them say
  almost anything else.

## Subtext over text
- ❌ *"I'm angry that you left and I felt abandoned."*
- ✅ *"You found your way back fast enough when there was something to inherit."*
- People attack sideways, joke when they're hurt, go quiet when it's worst. Let the surface be
  about one thing and the real fight be about another.

## Cut the throat-clearing
- People interrupt and overlap. They don't wait for full paragraphs.
- Trim greetings, "well," "you know," and recaps — unless a specific one reveals character.
- Start the scene late and leave early. We don't need hello and goodbye.

## Every branch *sounds* different (this is worldbuilding through voice)
- **Originists** — plain, bodily, grounded in birth/food/weather/death. Short when hurt.
- **Ascended (Ren)** — fast, witty, a half-step ahead; hides feeling in cleverness.
- **Digitals (Sela)** — open, patient, gently certain; the calm of someone who feels no fear.
- **The Made (Davin)** — even, precise, devastating softly; never raises its voice, never bites.
- **The Choir** — warm and *plural*; slides between "I remember" and "we believe" without a seam.
- **Elias** — slow, in small concrete pictures from a lost world; answers big with tiny.

If you covered the names, the reader should still know who's speaking. Test it.

## Dialogue tags
- "Said" and "asked" are nearly invisible — use them. Don't reach for "exclaimed," "retorted,"
  "interjected."
- Best of all: replace the tag with a small action that also shows feeling.
  - ❌ *"I'm fine," she said sadly.*
  - ✅ *"I'm fine." She turned the cup so the chip faced away.*
- Never explain the line with an adverb ("she said angrily"). If the line needs it, rewrite the
  line.

## Arguments (the engine of this book)
- Both sides must be **right.** Give the opponent the best version of their case, not a strawman.
- Let people score real hits. Let the POV character lose sometimes.
- The reader should be torn. If they're not, one side is too weak — fix it.

## Read it out loud, in character
If a line doesn't fit a real mouth, it dies. Speak it. If you'd never say it, neither would they.
