# Proverbs & Sayings — The Book's Folk Wisdom

A civilization five billion years old would not run on philosophy papers. It would run on
**sayings** — wisdom worn smooth by repetition, like the Stone's grip worn pale by thumbs.
Proverbs are the oldest surviving artifacts of any language, which makes them perfect for a story
about what survives. Used right, they do worldbuilding, characterization, and theme in one breath.
Used wrong, they're fortune cookies. This file keeps them right.

## The rules
1. **Diegetic only.** Every saying belongs to a people — the keepers, a branch, a faith. No
   imported Earth proverbs, with one exception: **Elias** may carry ancient ones, half-broken,
   their sources named as lost.
2. **Characters say them. The narrator never does.** A proverb in narration is the author
   preaching (see [anti-AI-tells](01-avoiding-ai-tells.md), rule E).
3. **One per chapter, at most.** A proverb is a spice. Two is a sermon.
4. **The best deployment is *against* the speaker or the scene.** A proverb confirmed is
   decoration; a proverb broken, ironic, or quoted as a weapon is drama. (Example: *"A keeper's
   hands are borrowed"* — said TO Yara after she claims the Stone.)
5. **Never let one state the core theme cleanly.** No saying may paraphrase "humanity survived
   everything except agreement on what humanity is." The theme is felt, not quoted.
6. **Broken sayings are encouraged.** Deep time eats endings. A proverb with its second half lost
   is the setting made audible — and lets the book pose questions it refuses to answer (Rule 7 in
   miniature).
7. **When a chapter mints one organically, log it here.** The manuscript is already doing this.

## The bank (unused — deploy when natural, never force)

### Keeper's sayings — House Vael and the Custodian line
*Working wisdom of people who hold things across deep time. Yara inherited these; she can quote
them dryly, resentfully, or have them quoted against her.*
- **"A keeper's hands are borrowed."** — you hold for the dead behind you and the unborn ahead;
  nothing you keep is yours. *Use: as a weapon against Yara after she claims the Stone — by a
  lesser House, or worse, by Sela.*
- **"Dust is also the house."** — keeping includes keeping the loss; decay is part of the trust.
  *Use: Yara cleaning; or defending the dead apple tree.*
- **"First find north. The hours will spend themselves."** — direction is chosen; time spends
  itself regardless. *(After an old Earth observation that sailors could find north before they
  could count hours.) Use: Elias or an old keeper text, when Yara is paralyzed by the deadline —
  the clock is not the decision.*
- **"The first duty is to be there in the morning."** — continuity as the whole job. *Use:
  smallest possible moment — exhaustion, not glory.*

### Elias — deep-time fragments (his alone)
*He carries sayings of vanished peoples. Sometimes the ending is gone. That is the point.*
- **The river saying (broken):** *"The water goes on; the river — "* and he has lost the ending.
  The proverb that decided whether a thing replaced drop by drop is still itself — and the verdict
  is gone from the universe. *Use: ONE scene, with Sela in earshot or in mind (Ch.6–7 candidate).
  It is the Continuity question with the answer eaten by time. NEVER restore the ending. Anyone
  who claims to know how it ends is telling on themselves.*
- **"Slow is also a speed."** — the Originist defense, in four words. *Use: against Ren or the
  Foremost, mid-argument, mildly.*
- **"Old roads know things."** — trust what has survived being walked on. *Use: about the forms,
  the rites, the law — shortly before the law fails them.*

### The Made — Foundry maxims
- **"Nobody thanks the bridge."** — they keep everything standing, unthanked; quiet grievance
  under the serenity. *Use: explains the lattice flicker's anger better than any speech — could be
  said by a Foundry voice, or bitterly quoted BY Yara at Davin.*
- **"Rust is honest. Paint is not."** — distrust of sentiment and surfaces. *Use: a Made delegate
  on the Originists' rituals.*
- **"What works, is."** — their entire metaphysics in three words; substrate irrelevance as folk
  wisdom. *Use: Davin, once, gently, about himself.*

### The Choir — liturgy
- **"A voice alone is a cry. Voices together are a place."** — home is plural; matches canon
  *"We are home. We brought it with us"* (Ch.5). *Use: the Choir explaining itself to someone
  about to join — terrifying because it's beautiful.*
- **"What is shared cannot be lost."** — the core promise. *Use: deploy straight in Book 1; break
  it in Book 2 ("We remember dark" — something WAS lost, or feared, inside the union).*

### The Meritocracy — the Foremost's school
- **"Being right is the only kindness that scales."** — why they rule by capability; compassion
  as competence. *Use: the Foremost, defending herself to Yara — and it should almost land.*
- **"A question delayed is a question answered by accident."** — her Ch.3 provocation in proverb
  form. *Use: when someone urges more waiting.*

### The Continued — Assembly sayings
- **"The original is the first copy."** — their whole philosophy; fighting words to an Originist.
  *Use: ONLY at maximum stakes — this one detonates the Yara↔Sela wound. Sela should never say it;
  someone cruder should, near her, and her flinch is the scene.*
- **"Forgetting is the only death."** — why they archive. *Use: ironic against Elias, who forgets
  daily and is the most alive thing in the book.*

### Children of Sol — funeral sayings of the Faithful Dead
- **"Every fire was lit from another fire."** — surface: the Sun as mother-fire, honor the source.
  Depth: the cosmological loop — a universe lit from a previous one (twist #10's rhyme, never
  confirmed). *Use: a pilgrim, simply, at the camps in the old sea beds. The double meaning is
  for re-readers only. Strong candidate for the climax's epigraph-moment.*
- **"Stand where the light can find you."** — presence as devotion; why they came. *Use: a
  pilgrim explaining, without defensiveness, why they will not leave.*
