# When and Where We Are

**The short version:** It is about 5 billion years from now. The universe is old and tired,
humanity is everywhere — and completely alone.

## A worn-out universe
Stars don't last forever. By this far in the future, most of the bright ones have burned out,
and barely any new ones are being born. The sky is darker, redder, and emptier than the one we
know. The universe isn't ending yet — but it's clearly past its best days, like a fire burning
down to embers.

## Humanity is ancient
People have lived among the stars for so long that almost none of their real history survives.
Billions of years. In that time humanity rose and fell many times over — great ages came and
went, and most of them are now half-forgotten legends. Nobody alive truly remembers how it all
began. That lost memory matters later.

## We are alone
Here is the strange, heavy fact at the center of everything: in all that time, across the
entire universe, humanity has **never found another living mind.** No aliens. Not one. Not even
the ruins of one. As far as anyone can prove, we are the only thinking creatures the universe
has ever made.

## The one clue
There is a single exception — and it isn't out among the stars. It's hidden in the universe
*itself*. Deep in the way reality is built, scientists keep finding a pattern that looks too
neat to be an accident — as if the very beginning of everything (the birth of the universe) may
have been *meant*, not random.

No one agrees what it means. Some say it proves we were created on purpose. Some say it's
nothing — we're just seeing shapes in the clouds. This argument is the root of every religion
in the story. (More in `/religions`.)

> **Why this matters:** In an empty universe, no one can tell us why we exist or what we're
> for. We have to answer that ourselves — and we can't agree. That pressure is what the whole
> series is really about.
