# Earth — The Last Cradle

**The short version:** The Sun is finally dying and will burn Earth to bare rock. Earth is the
one place life is known to have ever begun — so its death forces humanity to ask who really
owns where we came from.

## The dying Sun (the clock)
Our Sun has been quietly burning for billions of years. Now it's running out of fuel. As it
dies it swells up huge and blistering hot — and soon it will scorch Earth down to dead stone.

Everyone has always known this day would come. It was a far-off certainty, like knowing winter
comes. Now winter is here. **This is the clock the whole story runs on:** the Sun's death is
coming, no one can stop it, and it forces every decision.

## Why Earth still matters
Earth is the only place, anywhere in the universe, where life is known to have started on its
own. The single cradle. Every kind of human alive today — no matter how strange they've
become — can trace their family line all the way back to it.

So Earth isn't really a home anymore. Hardly anyone lives there. It's something bigger and
stranger: a **relic.** A shrine. The grave of where we began, and the proof that we began at
all. Losing it feels, to many, like losing the last piece of who they are.

## The Custodians
One family has guarded and cared for Earth for longer than most whole civilizations have lasted.
They are the **Custodians** *(working family name: House Vael)*. Their right to keep Earth is
ancient — once, no one would have dared question it.

But that right is fraying now. And when the dying Sun finally kills Earth, a dangerous question
opens up: **who inherits the homeworld — and the meaning that comes with it?** Every branch of
humanity has an answer, and they don't match.
