# How Power Is Won, and How Wars Are Fought

**The short version:** a civilization this old and this strange does not fight with rifles and
bombs over hills. It fights over **what counts as a human** — and the weapons are identity itself.
Get this wrong and the whole future shrinks back down to 21st-century war in fancy dress.

## First: what is actually scarce?
Conflict comes from scarcity. After five billion years, the scarce things are not land, metal, or
fuel. They are:

- **Attention.** The rarest resource of all is *consciousness* — who gets to think, how much, and
  for how long. Processing, memory, and time are rationed. To be granted more mind is to be made
  more powerful; to be starved of it is a quiet kind of death.
- **Legitimacy.** The real prize of the whole series. Not territory — **recognition.** The right
  to be called the true continuation of humanity. Whoever the others accept as the heir inherits
  everything that matters, and almost no one will grant it.
- **Access.** To the last relics, and above all to **Earth** — the cradle, and (it will turn out)
  the last unbroken place from which the deepest question can even be asked. Everyone wants a hand
  on the final, irreplaceable thing.

## War is identity warfare, not artillery
Crude force still exists — at the edges, as the lowest and most shameful resort. But the *real*
war between the branches is fought with the tools of their own natures, and the battlefield is
always the same question: **what counts as human?** Each branch wages it in the only way it can:

- **Digitals** copy and edit. Duplicate a rival's leader. Revise the record. Win the vote by
  replicating into it. *(Is the leader who signed the treaty the real one — or last night's copy?)*
- **The Choir** absorbs. It takes populations into the union, one ended loneliness at a time. *(Is
  a city that joined the Choir conquered, or saved, or simply gone?)*
- **The Made** rebuild and withhold. They remake sacred places into "better" ones, and they hold
  the off-switch on the systems others need to breathe. *(Is preserving Earth by machine still
  Earth — or a forgery that calls itself a rescue?)*
- **The Ascended** modify. They improve populations, and children, toward their own model, until
  the unmodified look diseased. *(Is uplifting a child love, or erasing the child you had?)*
- **The Originists** restrict and refuse. They close access to the relics and withhold the
  blessing of legitimacy. *(Is guarding the cradle stewardship — or a hand on the throat of
  everyone who wants in?)*

Every one of these is an attack on someone's *identity* or *origin*, not their body. That is the
point. The war is over the definition of the word *human*, and each side fights by trying to make
its definition the only one left standing.

## How this escalates (with the series)
- **Book 1 — cold.** Identity warfare in its quiet form: legitimacy fights, restricted access,
  the first unilateral *move* on Earth. No fleets burning yet. The threat of all of the above,
  held just under the surface of a debate.
- **Books 2–3 — hot.** The moves turn real. Copying, absorbing, modifying, withholding,
  rebuilding — done to people the reader loves, at scale.
- **Book 4+ — total.** The final form of "who inherits humanity," with Earth and the Sun as the
  stakes on the table.

> **The rule (constitution, Rule 8):** if you ever catch the war becoming a normal space-war —
> fleets, beam weapons, capture the planet — stop. That's the failure state. The war is fought
> over *recognition, substrate, and minds.* Keep it strange. Keep it about the word *human*.
